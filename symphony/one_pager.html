How I Think

Most operations hires are evaluated on which systems they've touched. I'd rather be evaluated on how I think, because the systems change every few years and the thinking is what transfers.

When I meet an established process, my instinct is archaeological — who built this, what were they solving for, and which assumptions have quietly expired. Most dysfunction isn't designed; it accumulates. I work at three focal lengths at once: the granular mechanics, the structural handoffs between departments, and what it costs a person to use a system that fights them. Thirteen years in advocacy and a working life at the piano taught me the same skill — hearing which signal matters and which noise is just crowding it.

Archaeology before architecture  ·  Three focal lengths  ·  Friction is data  ·  Built, not borrowed

A Master of Social Work (MSW) systems practitioner and nonprofit operations leader with ten-plus years across advocacy, government, and housing. Resident pianist at The Jefferson Hotel since 2022.
