How the concept works

From trusted records to explainable action

This prototype imagines a governed decision-support layer around existing systems. It does not replace Bloomerang or Finance, and this demo has no live connections.

Select data · Ask AI
1

Connect approved sources

Read-only CRM exports or APIs, Finance reconciliation files, campaign goals, and approved engagement data arrive with source, permission, and refresh timestamps.

Validate and calculate

Gift, household, designation, pledge, and fund identifiers are standardized. Deterministic formulas calculate the dashboard; Finance exceptions remain visible instead of being silently overwritten.

Explain, decide, document

AI translates trusted metrics into questions and suggested next steps. Staff review the evidence, record context, and decide what—if anything—should return to the source workflow.

How I built this with AI

I used AI as a design, research, and coding partner—not as an autonomous decision-maker. I defined the interview goal, supplied the job description and application materials, chose the features, challenged weak or overly generic outputs, and reviewed each iteration. The AI helped me turn those decisions into a working prototype quickly.

Step 1

Translate the role into requirements

I mapped the job description to concrete product areas: reporting, retention, moves management, stewardship, CRM governance, Finance, and staff development.

Step 2

Set the safety boundary

I required fictional demonstration data, visible disclaimers, a protected access page, and no connection to Richmond Symphony, Bloomerang, or real donor records.

Step 3

Model realistic decisions

I used credible fundraising patterns and real-world arts budget pressures to shape scenarios, then kept the figures illustrative rather than presenting estimates as facts.

Step 4

Prototype the experience

AI helped build the responsive interface, calculations, charts, scripted assistant, private notes, and browser-local task queue from my product direction.

Step 5

Review through annotated feedback

I clicked specific parts of the live site, explained what was unclear or too generic, and used that feedback to improve hierarchy, language, analysis, and workflow.

Step 6

Test, explain, and publish

I checked the access gate, interactions, downloadable copy, responsive behavior, and prototype disclosures before publishing the revised private site.
